\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Models.}
All models share one spiking world model, the network of \swm~\cite{sun2025spikingwm} built on DreamerV3 at the size of DreamerV3-S (512 units, $T=5$), and differ in the recurrent core:
\textbf{\swm} (multicompartment neurons with both resets, $\tau=2$);
\textbf{recurrent LIF} neurons, cleared at every environment step as usual in spiking RL;
\textbf{GRSN}~\cite{qin2025grsn}, gated recurrent spiking neurons;
\textbf{DH-SNN}~\cite{zheng2024dhsnn}, LIF neurons with eight dendritic branches of learned time constants;
\textbf{TC-LIF}~\cite{zhang2024tclif}, two-compartment LIF neurons for long sequences (GRSN, DH-SNN and TC-LIF update once per environment step and carry their state);
\textbf{\dv}~\cite{hafner2025dreamerv3}, the same architecture without spikes (GRU core, SiLU, one simulation step);
and \textbf{\method} with two PDN layers.
R2I~\cite{samsami2024r2i}, a DreamerV3 agent with a state space recurrence and the state of the art among ANN world models on memory tasks, runs with its official code and configuration on POPGym; on BSuite we compare with its published results.
Within a benchmark, all models use identical hyperparameters, budgets and evaluation.
The actor and critic of every model read $(z_t,h_t)$; those of \method also read its dendritic state as spikes (\cref{sec:wm}), with 4.1M instead of 3.4M parameters (sizes in Supp.~\suppref{supp:impl}{A}, an actor-critic of 3.4M in Supp.~\suppref{supp:policy}{E}).

\paragraph{Benchmarks.}
We use benchmarks with published protocols and ANN results.
\emph{BSuite memory length}~\cite{osband2020bsuite} shows a cue, then $L$ uninformative steps, and rewards the agent for recalling the cue; we run $L\in\{8,16,32,64\}$, and $L\in\{51,71,101\}$ with the lengths, budget (1M steps) and training windows of R2I~\cite{samsami2024r2i}.
\emph{POPGym RepeatPrevious}~\cite{morad2023popgym} rewards the agent at every step for naming the suit of the card shown $k$ steps earlier ($k=4$ Easy, $k=32$ Medium), one of the three POPGym tasks with the highest memory demand that R2I evaluates; we use the environment settings of R2I's code (eight parallel environments, train ratio 64, unscaled rewards) for 2M steps.
\emph{Memory Maze 9$\times$9}~\cite{pasukonis2023memorymaze} is first-person 3D navigation with episodes of 1000 steps; we use its offline probing benchmark with the official dataset and probes: after 1M world model updates with truncated backpropagation through time, probes decode the positions of the target objects (MSE) and the maze layout (accuracy) from the frozen state.
\emph{DMC}~\cite{tassa2018dmc} from proprioception (walker walk, cheetah run, cartpole swingup; 500k steps, train ratio 512, as in DreamerV3) and walker walk from pixels (1M frames) serve as control tasks without a memory demand.
On BSuite the reward depends on cue \emph{and} action, which the scalar reward head of a Dreamer world model does not learn within our budgets; all models therefore use a reward head with one output per action (Supp.~\suppref{supp:reward}{B}).

\paragraph{World model probes.}
To measure memory without exploration and credit assignment, we also train world models alone on data of a random policy and measure the AUC of their reward prediction where the reward depends on the past (\cref{sec:causal}): \emph{cue recall}, where the terminal reward is the cue $L$ steps back, and \emph{RepeatPrevious}, where the reward indicates whether the action names the card $k$ steps back.
As in probing benchmarks~\cite{pasukonis2023memorymaze}, we report the final AUC after a fixed budget of 12k (cue recall) or 10k (RepeatPrevious) updates; Supp.~\suppref{supp:probe}{C} gives the updates until the AUC exceeds 0.9.

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
Evaluations average 32 episodes on BSuite (every 10k steps, 20k with R2I's lengths) and 16 on POPGym (every 50k), with the mode of the policy; Supp.~\suppref{supp:impl}{A} reports how the return holds after it first reaches 0.9.

\label{sec:rl}

\Cref{tab:rl}, \cref{fig:memory_length} and \cref{fig:rl_curves} show how far each model remembers.
The spiking baselines stop where their neurons lose state.
\swm, cleared at every step and at every spike, recalls no cue, not even 8 steps back.
Recurrent LIF neurons, cleared at every step, solve 8 steps; GRSN, which carries its state but resets its membrane at every spike, solves 16, and so does DH-SNN.
TC-LIF carries both compartments across steps, and its spikes subtract a fixed amount from them instead of clearing them; it solves 64 steps, as the design rule of \cref{sec:amnesia} predicts.
\dv solves up to 32 steps in the short budgets and 51 steps with R2I's lengths (after 902k of 1M steps), and fails at 64, 71 and 101.

\method reaches a return of 0.9 at every length.
Up to 64 steps it is as fast as the fastest baseline or faster (64 steps after \numFwfToSixtyFour\ environment steps, TC-LIF after 112k), and with R2I's lengths it learns 51, 71 and 101 steps after \numFwfToFiftyOne, \numFwfToSeventyOne\ and \numFwfToHundredOne\ steps (returns in Supp.~\suppref{supp:impl}{A}).
R2I reports a median return of about 0.9 at 51 and 71 memory steps and about 0.35 at 101 after 1M steps (10 seeds; Fig.~7 of~\cite{samsami2024r2i}), with DreamerV3's default reward head and lengths that count one step less.

On RepeatPrevious Easy, \method reaches \numFwfEasy, close to the best baseline, recurrent LIF (0.69).
On Medium, which asks for the card of exactly 32 steps ago, every baseline, \dv included, stays at chance after 2M steps, while \method reaches \numFwfMedium.
R2I, run with its official code, stays at chance on both tasks within 2M steps.
In its published curves over 50M steps, R2I leaves chance on Medium after about 3M steps and DreamerV3 learns Easy but not Medium (Fig.~9 of~\cite{samsami2024r2i}), as in our runs; \method learns Medium within a budget in which R2I has not yet left chance.

The probes separate memory from policy learning (\cref{tab:probe}).
On cue recall, \method and TC-LIF recall 64 steps (AUC 1.00); DH-SNN and \dv stop at 32, GRSN at 16, recurrent LIF at 8, and \swm recalls no length.
The order is that of RL, so the limits of the baselines are limits of their world models.

RepeatPrevious asks for a different memory: the input of an exact lag among a stream of similar inputs.
At $k=4$ most models learn it (AUC 0.86 to 0.99; \method 0.98).
At $k=32$, \method is the only model that learns it (AUC 0.92); all others, TC-LIF and \dv included, stay at 0.50 to 0.51.
Resonance is the reason: without resonant channels the same model reaches only 0.57 (\cref{tab:ablation}).
We added resonant channels after an earlier model with integrator channels only failed this task (Supp.~\suppref{supp:diag}{F}) and used RepeatPrevious Hard ($k=64$) for no design choice.
On Hard, \method reaches an AUC of 0.94, and all other models stay at 0.50 to 0.57.

\subsection{Ablations}
\label{sec:ablation}

\begin{table}[t]
  \centering\tabfont
  \caption{\textbf{Ablations} on the world model probes (as in \cref{tab:probe}). Each row changes one design choice of \method.}
  \label{tab:ablation}
  \input{tables/ablation}
\end{table}

\Cref{tab:ablation} changes one design choice at a time (updates until 0.9 in Supp.~\suppref{supp:probe}{C}).
\emph{Persistence}: clearing the dendrites at every environment step erases the memory (AUC 0.50 to 0.51 on all three tasks), the direct test of reset amnesia inside our own model.
\emph{Retention time}: a single retention time of 16 steps fails at 64 steps and one of 128 succeeds; the spectrum from 1 to 1000 steps succeeds without being set for the task, learned or kept at its initialization.
\emph{Write gain}: with the Euler gain $1-\lambda$ the channels with long retention times are too faint, and the model fails at 64 steps (0.52) and reaches only 0.76 at $k=32$.
\emph{Stateless soma}: a soma that keeps a membrane potential and is reset by its spike fails at 64 steps (0.52) and reaches 0.77 at $k=32$.
\emph{Resonance}: without resonant channels the model fails at $k=32$ (0.57); with resonant channels only it solves all three tasks, as does \method with one channel of each kind.
\emph{Depth}: one PDN layer fails at 64 steps; with one layer and one simulation step it recalls 64 steps but no longer the exact lag (0.50).
\emph{Sparsity}: capping the firing rate at 0.06 (from 0.25) keeps 32 steps and the exact lag but loses 64 steps within the budget.
The spike count, the feedback and the number of channels leave the memory nearly unchanged (AUC 0.88 to 0.94 at $k=32$); the spike count and the feedback serve the prediction of motion (\cref{sec:why}).

Per environment step (Supp.~\suppref{supp:energy}{H}), the agent of \method, world model filter and actor, costs \numEnergyAgent\,$\mu$J, \numEnergyRatio$\times$ less than \dv (\numEnergyAgentDv\,$\mu$J); the filter alone costs \numEnergyFilter\ against \numEnergyFilterDv\,$\mu$J, at a recurrent firing rate of \numEnergyRate.
Most of its operations are accumulates triggered by spikes, including those of the 3072 dendritic spike inputs of the actor (\numEnergyActor\,$\mu$J for the whole actor); the multiply-accumulates of the dendrites stay inside each neuron.
The spiking baselines cost \numEnergySpiking\,$\mu$J.
As in SNN energy reports, the estimates count 32-bit arithmetic~\cite{horowitz2014energy} and leave out memory access, which can dominate on hardware~\cite{dampfhoffer2023energy}, for all models.
