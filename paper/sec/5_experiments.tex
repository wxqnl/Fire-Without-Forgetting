\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Models.}
All models share one spiking world model, the DreamerV3-style network of \swm~\cite{sun2025spikingwm} at the size of DreamerV3-S (512 units, $T=5$), and differ only in the recurrent core:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{\swm}~\cite{sun2025spikingwm}: multi-compartment neurons with both resets, $\tau=2$;
\item \textbf{Recurrent LIF}: LIF neurons with recurrent spike input, cleared at every environment step, the usual practice in spiking RL;
\item \textbf{GRSN}~\cite{qin2025grsn}: gated recurrent spiking neurons, updated once per environment step with their state carried across steps;
\item \textbf{\swmnr}: \swm with interventions A+B+G of \cref{sec:causal} and $\tau_d=128$, \ie, with both resets removed;
\item \textbf{\dv}~\cite{hafner2025dreamerv3}: the same architecture without spikes (GRU core, SiLU activations, one simulation step), an ANN reference;
\item \textbf{\method}: two PDN layers (\cref{sec:method}).
\end{itemize}
As the state of the art among ANN world models on memory tasks we also report \textbf{R2I}~\cite{samsami2024r2i}, a DreamerV3 agent with a state-space recurrence, run with its official code and configuration on POPGym, and with its published results on BSuite.
Within a benchmark, all models use identical hyperparameters, budgets and evaluation and differ only in the recurrent core and, for \method on POPGym, in the policy input (\cref{sec:wm}; Supp.~\suppref{supp:impl}{A}).
On POPGym, \method has 8.6M world-model parameters, \dv 7.5M and \swm 7.2M.

\paragraph{Benchmarks.}
We use benchmarks with published protocols and ANN results.
\emph{BSuite memory length}~\cite{osband2020bsuite} shows a cue, then $L$ uninformative steps, and rewards the agent for recalling the cue; we run $L\in\{8,16,32,64\}$ and $L\in\{51,71,101\}$ under the protocol of R2I~\cite{samsami2024r2i}.
\emph{POPGym RepeatPrevious}~\cite{morad2023popgym} rewards the agent at every step for naming the suit of the card shown $k$ steps earlier ($k=4$ Easy, $k=32$ Medium), one of the three most memory-demanding POPGym tasks that R2I evaluates; we use the environment settings of R2I's code (eight parallel environments, train ratio 64, unscaled rewards) for 2M steps.
\emph{Memory Maze 9$\times$9}~\cite{pasukonis2023memorymaze} is first-person 3D navigation with 1000-step episodes; we use its offline probing benchmark with the official dataset and probes: after 1M world-model updates with truncated backpropagation through time, probes decode the positions of the target objects (MSE) and the maze layout (accuracy) from the frozen state.
\emph{DMC}~\cite{tassa2018dmc} from proprioception (walker walk, cheetah run, cartpole swingup; 500k steps, train ratio 512, as in DreamerV3) is a non-memory control on which \swm was evaluated.
On BSuite the reward is a function of cue \emph{and} action, which a scalar reward head of a Dreamer world model fails to learn within our budgets; all models therefore use a reward head indexed by the action (Supp.~\suppref{supp:reward}{B}).
This head also helps \dv, which solves 51 steps here, whereas R2I reported it failing beyond about 30.

\paragraph{World-model probes.}
To measure memory without exploration and credit assignment, we also train world models alone on random-policy data and measure the AUC of their reward prediction where reward depends on the past (\cref{sec:causal}): \emph{memlen-$L$-recall}, where the terminal reward is the cue $L$ steps back, and \emph{RepeatPrevious}, where the reward indicates whether the action names the card $k$ steps back.
We report updates until the AUC exceeds 0.9 within 12k (memlen) or 10k (RepeatPrevious) updates.

\subsection{Memory in reinforcement learning}
\label{sec:rl}

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig/rl_curves}
  \caption{\textbf{Learning curves} on BSuite memory length under the protocol of R2I (left three) and POPGym RepeatPrevious (right two; chance $=-0.5$). Exponential moving average of the evaluation return (R2I: training-episode return, see \cref{tab:rl}). \pend{\method's runs are in progress.}}
  \label{fig:rl_curves}
\end{figure*}

\Cref{tab:rl}, \cref{fig:memory_length} and \cref{fig:rl_curves} show that every spiking baseline stops at a memory length set by its resets.
\swm never recalls a cue, not even 8 steps back.
Recurrent LIF, cleared at every step, solves 8 steps; GRSN, which keeps its state but resets its membrane at every spike, solves 16; \swmnr, without both resets, solves 32.
\dv solves up to 32 steps in the short budgets and 51 steps in 902k of 1M steps under R2I's protocol, and fails at 64, 71 and 101.

\method solves every length.
It is as fast as the best baseline up to 16 steps, the fastest at 32 (32k steps), and the only model that solves 64 steps (102k); after that its return stays at or above 0.5 (final 0.73), whereas no other model exceeds 0.5 at any evaluation.
Under R2I's protocol it solves 51, 71 and 101 steps in 82k, 282k and 362k environment steps; \dv needs 902k steps for 51 and solves neither 71 nor 101.
R2I reports a median return of about 0.9 at 51 and 71 memory steps and about 0.35 at 101 after 1M steps (10 seeds; Fig.~7 of~\cite{samsami2024r2i}), with DreamerV3's default reward head; our lengths count one step more, and all our models use the action-indexed reward head (\cref{sec:setup}), so the comparison is indicative.

On RepeatPrevious Easy, \method reaches a return of \numFwfEasy\ after \numFwfEasyProgress\ of the budget, while the final returns of the baselines range from $-0.29$ (\swmnr) to 0.69 (recurrent LIF).
On Medium, all baselines remain at chance after 2M steps, \dv included, consistent with R2I~\cite{samsami2024r2i}, whereas the return of \method has risen to \numFwfMedium\ after \numFwfMediumProgress\ of the budget.

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig/probe_panels}
  \caption{\textbf{Persistence, retention time, write gain and resonance} (world-model probes, AUC of the reward prediction; 0.5 = no memory).
  (a) Variants of \method recalling a cue 64 steps back: clearing the dendrites at every step, retention times shorter than the memory, or the Euler write gain all leave the world model without memory.
  (b) Recalling the card of exactly 32 steps ago: only \method with resonant channels learns it.}
  \label{fig:probe}

The probes separate memory from policy learning (\cref{tab:probe}).
On memlen-$L$-recall, \method learns every length at least as fast as any other model and is the only one that recalls 64 steps (3.5k updates); \dv and \swmnr stop at 32, GRSN at 16, LIF at 8.
The same ordering as in RL shows that the limits of the baselines are limits of their world models.

RepeatPrevious asks for a different memory: not one value held for long, but the input of an exact lag among a stream of similar inputs.
At $k=4$ most models learn it, \method fastest (2k updates).
At $k=32$, \method is the only model that learns it (AUC 0.93 after 6k updates); all others, including \dv and GRSN, remain at 0.50--0.55 (\cref{fig:probe}b).
Resonance is the reason: the same model with integrator channels only also stays at chance (0.54).
In RL, the world model of the earlier integrator-only \method reached a reward AUC of only 0.55 on Medium evaluation episodes, so its policy had nothing to act on (Supp.~\suppref{supp:diag}{F}).

\subsection{Ablations}
\label{sec:ablation}

\begin{table}[t]
  \centering\tabfont
  \caption{\textbf{Ablations} on the world-model probes (updates until AUC $>0.9$; -- (AUC) = not within 12k/10k updates). Each row changes one design choice of \method; a final AUC after an update count marks a run that crossed 0.9 but ended below it.}
  \label{tab:ablation}
  \input{tables/ablation}
\end{table}

\Cref{tab:ablation} and \cref{fig:probe}a remove one design choice at a time.
\emph{Persistence}: clearing the dendrites at every environment step erases the memory of \method (AUC 0.50 at 64 steps), the direct test of reset-induced amnesia inside our own model.
\emph{Retention time}: a single retention time of 16 steps fails at 64 steps, while 128 steps succeeds; the log-uniform spectrum succeeds without being set for the task, and learning it changes little against keeping its initialization (3.5k vs.\ 4k updates).
\emph{Write gain}: with the Euler gain $1-\lambda$ the long-retention channels are too faint, and the model fails at 64 steps.
\emph{Depth}: one PDN layer fails at 64 steps; the spikes of the first layer give the second a nonlinear input.
\emph{Channel types}: without resonant channels the model fails RepeatPrevious Medium; with only resonant channels it still solves it but needs almost twice as many updates at 64 steps (6.5k vs.\ 3.5k, and its AUC does not stay above 0.9), where a value must be held unchanged.
Half of each kind keeps both strengths.
\emph{Sparsity}: capping the firing rate at 0.06 (from 0.18) keeps 32-step recall (2k updates) but loses 64-step recall within the budget.

On DMC (\cref{tab:mm}), \method reaches \pend{[--]}, \pend{[--]} and \pend{[--]} on walker walk, cheetah run and cartpole swingup, against 923, 343 and 497 for \swm and 953, 699 and 871 for \dv.

Per environment step, the world-model filter of \method costs 5.9\,$\mu$J, 2.9$\times$ less than \dv (17.2\,$\mu$J), at a recurrent firing rate of 0.16.
Most of its operations are accumulates triggered by spikes; the multiply-accumulates of the dendrites are local to each neuron (3 per integrator and 6 per resonant channel and step).
Recurrent LIF and GRSN are cheaper still (4.9 and 3.6\,$\mu$J) but do not remember beyond 8 and 16 steps.
These estimates count arithmetic only, the convention of SNN energy reports; memory access, which a hardware-aware study shows can dominate~\cite{dampfhoffer2023energy}, is not included for any model.
