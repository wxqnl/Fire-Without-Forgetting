\section{Reset-Induced Amnesia}
\label{sec:amnesia}

\subsection{Spiking world models}
\label{sec:swm}

We follow the recurrent state-space model of DreamerV3~\cite{hafner2025dreamerv3}.
At environment step $t$ a recurrent core updates a deterministic state $h_t$ from $h_{t-1}$, the previous stochastic latent $z_{t-1}$ and action $a_{t-1}$; the posterior infers $z_t$ from $h_t$ and the observation embedding, the prior predicts it from $h_t$ alone, and decoder, reward and continuation heads read $(h_t, z_t)$.
In a spiking world model~\cite{sun2025spikingwm} every layer is spiking and is simulated for $T$ steps per environment step ($T=5$), and $h_t\in\{0,1\}^{T\times N}$ is the spike output of the core.

\swm uses multi-compartment neurons (MCNs).
In simulation step $k$ of environment step $t$, a basal and an apical dendrite integrate synaptic input with Euler-discretized leaks, and the soma integrates the basal potential gated by the apical one:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
v^{c}[k] &= v^{c}[k-1] + \tfrac{1}{\tau_c}\big(I^{c}[k] - v^{c}[k-1]\big), \quad c\in\{\mathrm{bas},\mathrm{api}\},\\
s[k] &= \Hv\big(v^{\mathrm{som}}[k] - \vartheta\big),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:mcn}
\end{equation}
where $I^{c}[k] = W_c\,[\,x_t[k];\,h_{t-1}[k]\,]$ combines the current input spikes with the core's spikes of the previous environment step and $\Hv$ is the Heaviside step, trained with a surrogate derivative~\cite{neftci2019surrogate,wu2018stbp}.
Two resets act on this neuron:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Step reset.} All compartments are set to zero at the start of every environment step.
\item \textbf{Spike reset.} When the soma fires, the soma and both dendrites are set to zero.\footnote{The equations of~\cite{sun2025spikingwm} show the somatic reset; the dendritic reset is part of its official implementation (\texttt{MCNode.calc\_spike}), which all our \swm runs use.}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Consequences.}
With the step reset, the core computes $h_t = \Phi(x_t, h_{t-1})$ from a zero state: it is a binary recurrent network, and everything it knows about the past must be re-encoded into the $T\times N$ bits of $h_{t-1}$ at every step.
The gradient from a reward $L$ steps after a cue crosses $L$ surrogate derivatives.
The time constants $\tau_c$ act only within the $T$ simulation steps of one environment step, which explains why changing them barely changes the performance of \swm~\cite{sun2025spikingwm}.
The spike reset additionally truncates a dendritic trace at the next spike: with $\alpha=1-1/\tau_c$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial v^{c}[k]}{\partial I^{c}[k-j]} = \frac{1}{\tau_c}\,\alpha^{j}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\big(1-s[k-i]\big),
\label{eq:spike_reset}
\end{equation}
which vanishes as soon as the neuron has fired once, so the effective memory is the shorter of $\tau_c$ and the inter-spike interval.

\paragraph{Write gain.}
Removing both resets does not make long time constants useful by itself.
The Euler update in \cref{eq:mcn} writes input with gain $1/\tau_c$, so for white input the stationary spread of $v^{c}$ shrinks as $1/\sqrt{2\tau_c}$: a long time constant retains a cue but stores it faintly.
With both resets removed (\cref{sec:causal}), a cue moved the basal potential of a $\tau_c=128$ dendrite by 0.004--0.009, about 1\% of the threshold $\vartheta=0.5$, against 0.19 at $\tau_c=2$.

\label{sec:causal}

We test these mechanisms with interventions on \swm in a world-model probe that isolates memory from exploration and credit assignment.
The data are BSuite memory-length episodes~\cite{osband2020bsuite} collected by a random policy, with the terminal reward set to the cue ($\pm1$, \emph{memlen-$L$-recall}); only the world model is trained, and we measure the AUC of its reward prediction at the recall step on held-out episodes, from 0.5 without memory to 1 with perfect recall (details in Supp.~\suppref{supp:probe}{C}).
Three interventions target the mechanisms above: \textbf{A} keeps all compartments across environment steps and clears them only at episode starts; \textbf{B} keeps the dendrites when the soma fires; \textbf{G} multiplies the dendritic input by $\sqrt{\tau_d/2}$, which equalizes the stationary spread across dendritic time constants $\tau_d$.
\Cref{tab:interventions} gives four findings.

\emph{With resets, time constants do not matter.}
\swm fails at 8 and 16 steps for $\tau_d\in\{2,16,128\}$, and the cue can be decoded from its spikes for only 2--3 steps after it is shown.
The one exception supports this diagnosis: at 32 steps, one of two seeds crosses an AUC of 0.9 at the last of 12k updates by holding the cue in self-sustained recurrent firing, a memory that exists only as spikes and is learned slowly and unreliably.

\emph{Persistence is necessary.}
Without A, neither B nor G helps.
With A and $\tau_d=128$, the world model recalls 8 and 16 steps; removing the dendritic reset as well (A+B) nearly halves the updates needed at 16 steps (4.5k instead of 8--8.5k).

\emph{The write gain limits long time constants.}
A+B fails at 32 steps although a linear probe still decodes the cue from the membrane potentials at the recall step (accuracy 0.88): the trace is there, but too faint to drive spikes.
Restoring the gain (A+B+G) recalls 8 and 16 steps within 500 updates and 32 steps within 2000--3000, faster than the GRU core.
The gain does not work by raising the firing rate: capped at a firing rate of 0.06, A+B+G still learns 32 steps in 2000 updates.

\emph{Removing resets is not sufficient for longer memory.}
A+B+G fails at 64 steps even with $\tau_d=512$, and in reinforcement learning it stops at 32 steps (\swmnr in \cref{fig:memory_length}).
Its dendrites still receive the core's spikes, so the recurrence is nonlinear and passes through the threshold at every step; its time constants are counted in simulation steps; and its soma still resets.
\Cref{sec:method} removes these remaining obstacles by design.
