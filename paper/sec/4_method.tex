\Cref{sec:amnesia} gives a design rule: memory must live in a state that spikes never reset, written with a gain that does not vanish for long time constants, and its recurrence should not pass through a threshold.
We build \method from neurons that follow this rule, keeping the rest of the spiking world model unchanged (\cref{fig:overview}).

\subsection{Persistent dendritic neuron}
\label{sec:pdn}

A layer has $N$ neurons with $M$ dendritic channels each.
Its input at environment step $t$ is the firing rate $\xx_t$ of the incoming spikes over the $T$ simulation steps, and the synaptic current into the dendrites is $\vect{w}_t = \LN(B\xx_t)\in\mathbb{R}^{NM}$, where $B$ is a synaptic matrix applied to spikes and $\LN$ is layer normalization~\cite{ba2016layernorm}.
Each channel has a retention time $\tau$, in environment steps, and a decay $\lambda=e^{-1/\tau}$.

\paragraph{Integrator channels} add the current to a leaky trace,
\begin{equation}
d_t = \lambda\, d_{t-1} + \sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}}\; w_t .
\label{eq:integrator}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Resonant channels} are damped oscillators with two state variables that rotate by a phase $\theta$ at every step:
\begin{equation}
\begin{pmatrix} a_t \\ b_t \end{pmatrix}
= \lambda \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix} a_{t-1} \\ b_{t-1} \end{pmatrix}
+ \sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}} \begin{pmatrix} w_t \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:resonant}
\end{equation}
An integrator channel is the special case $\theta=0$ with $b\equiv0$.

\paragraph{The soma} has no state. It reads the channels of its neuron and the direct input, and emits one spike per environment step:
\begin{equation}
u_t = \sum_{m=1}^{M} \big(C_m a_{t,m} + C'_m b_{t,m}\big) + (D\xx_t),\qquad
s_t = \Hv\big(\mathrm{N}(u_t) - \vartheta\big),
\label{eq:soma}
\end{equation}
where $C_m, C'_m$ are per-neuron weights, $D$ is a synaptic matrix, $\mathrm{N}$ is the normalization used throughout the world model and $\vartheta=0.5$.
The spike $s_t$ is repeated over the $T$ simulation steps and is the only signal the neuron sends to other neurons of the world model.

\paragraph{Parameters.}
We use $N=512$ and $M=4$, half of the channels of every neuron resonant.
Retention times are initialized log-uniformly in $[1,1000]$ environment steps and phases uniformly in $[0,2\pi)$ (the initialization of the LRU~\cite{orvieto2023lru}); both are learned.
The Heaviside step is trained with a surrogate derivative, and all states are reset only at the start of an episode.

\subsection{Why the neuron remembers}
\label{sec:why}

\paragraph{No reset in the memory path.}
The influence of a current $k$ steps back on an integrator channel is $\partial d_t/\partial w_{t-k} = \sqrt{1-\lambda^2}\,\lambda^{k}$, and on a resonant channel $\sqrt{1-\lambda^2}\,\lambda^{k} R(k\theta)$, whatever the neuron fired in between.
The memory and its gradient decay with the retention time alone and never cross a surrogate derivative on their way through time.
The spike, which does cross one, is recomputed from the dendrites at every step instead of carrying the memory forward.

\paragraph{Normalized write.}
For unit-variance white input, \cref{eq:integrator} gives $\mathrm{Var}(d_t)=(1-\lambda^2)\sum_{k\ge0}\lambda^{2k}=1$ for every $\tau$, whereas the Euler gain $1-\lambda$ of \cref{eq:mcn} gives $(1-\lambda)/(1+\lambda)\approx 1/(2\tau)$.
The normalization of the LRU~\cite{orvieto2023lru} thus keeps channels with retention times from 1 to 1000 steps on the same scale; intervention G is the same scaling up to a factor of 2.

\paragraph{A spectrum of retention times.}
The memory length a task needs is not known in advance.
Log-uniform retention times cover lengths from one to a thousand steps in every neuron, so no time constant is tuned per task.

\paragraph{Resonance tells time.}
An input that arrived $k$ steps ago contributes $\lambda^{k}(\cos k\theta,\ \sin k\theta)\,w_{t-k}$ to a resonant channel: its phase identifies the lag.
In integrator channels, inputs of different ages are superposed with smoothly decaying weights $\lambda^{j}$, so isolating the input of exactly $k$ steps ago from those of $k\pm1$ steps ago requires subtracting channels with nearby retention times, an ill-conditioned readout.
Integrators hold \emph{what} was seen, resonators also record \emph{when}.

\paragraph{Spikes communicate.}
All signals between neurons are spikes, so the synaptic operations $B\xx_t$ and $D\xx_t$ are accumulates.
The dendritic recurrence and the readout $\sum_m C_m d_m$ are local to a neuron, and the normalizations need only the mean and variance of a layer, as in every spiking world model we compare: $O(NM)$ multiply-accumulates per step, against $O(N^2)$ for a dense recurrent matrix.
Inside the world model, graded values never cross a synapse; they remain inside the neuron, as state variables of neuromorphic cores that support multi-variable neuron models~\cite{orchard2021loihi2}.

\subsection{Spiking world model}
\label{sec:wm}

\method replaces the recurrent core of the spiking world model (\cref{sec:swm}) by two PDN layers; the second layer reads the spikes of the first, and its spikes form the deterministic state $h_t$.
Everything else is unchanged and spiking: the input layer that reads $(z_{t-1},a_{t-1})$, the encoder, the posterior and prior over $z_t$ (32 categorical variables with 32 classes), and the decoder, reward and continuation heads, all built from LIF neurons with $T=5$ simulation steps, trained with the losses of DreamerV3~\cite{hafner2025dreamerv3}.
The dendritic state is carried through training sequences and through imagination rollouts, and is cleared only at episode starts.

\paragraph{Policy input.}
The actor and critic read $z_t$, $h_t$ and the state of the second-layer dendrites, on every task; the Memory Maze probes read the same features.
The soma sends one bit per neuron and step, whereas the dendrites hold the memory in graded form.
To pass them on as spikes, each state variable is standardized over the layer and encoded by $T$ ON and $T$ OFF thresholds at $\pm(k-\tfrac12)\tfrac{2}{T}$, $k=1,\dots,T$, giving $2T$ binary frames whose rates represent $2T+1$ levels.
With six state variables per neuron, the actor and critic receive 6144 additional binary inputs per simulation step, the only path by which the dendritic state leaves a neuron; their first layer reads these inputs through a rank-64 factor.
Supp.~\suppref{supp:policy}{E} compares a policy that reads $(z_t,h_t)$ only.
