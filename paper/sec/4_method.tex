\section{Spike-Driven Dreamer}
\label{sec:method}

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig/overview}
  \caption{\textbf{\methodfull.}
  (a) Two layers of persistent dendritic neurons (PDNs) form the recurrent core of a spiking Dreamer. Each neuron has an integrator (blue) and a resonant (orange) dendritic channel; PDM carries them across environment steps without reset, and NWG writes into them on one scale for every retention time. Its stateless soma sends a spike count over the $T=5$ simulation steps, and the spikes of a layer also feed back into its dendrites ($R$). The actor and critic also read the dendrites of the second layer as ON and OFF spikes.
  (b) In \swm, all compartments are cleared at every step and the dendrites at every somatic spike, so only spikes reach the next step. In a PDN, PDM keeps the dendritic channels across steps; spikes add to them but never clear them.}
  \label{fig:overview}
\end{figure*}

\method follows the design rule of \cref{sec:amnesia}: it keeps the spiking world model of \cref{sec:swm} and replaces its recurrent core with two layers of persistent dendritic neurons (PDNs), as shown in \cref{fig:overview}.
The second layer reads the spikes of the first, and its spikes form the deterministic state $h_t$; the encoder, the latent, the heads and the actor-critic remain LIF networks trained with the losses of DreamerV3~\cite{hafner2025dreamerv3}.

A PDN separates memory from communication: dendritic channels store the memory, and a stateless soma communicates it by spikes.
A layer has $N$ neurons with $M$ dendritic channels each.
At environment step $t$ it receives the firing rate $x_t$ of its input spikes over the $T$ simulation steps and its own firing rate $\bar s_{t-1}$ of the previous step, and computes
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
I_t &= \LN\big(B x_t + R\,\bar s_{t-1}\big),\\
(d_t,\tilde d_t) &= \mathrm{PDM}\big(d_{t-1},\tilde d_{t-1}; I_t\big),\\
s_t &= \mathrm{Soma}\big(d_t,\tilde d_t, x_t\big),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:input}
\end{equation}
where $B$ and $R$ are synaptic matrices, $\LN$ is layer normalization with learned scale and bias~\cite{ba2016layernorm}, $I_t\in\mathbb{R}^{NM}$ is the synaptic current into the dendrites, $(d_t,\tilde d_t)$ is the state of the dendritic channels and $s_t$ are the $T$ binary spike frames of the layer.
Persistent Dendritic Memory (\cref{sec:pdm}) carries the dendritic state across environment steps and somatic spikes, the Normalized Write Gain (\cref{sec:nwg}) writes the current into channels of every retention time on one scale, and the stateless soma (\cref{sec:soma}) turns the state into spike counts.
All operations act elementwise on the neurons and channels of a layer.

\subsection{Persistent Dendritic Memory}
\label{sec:pdm}

In \swm, the step reset clears all compartments at every environment step and the spike reset clears the dendrites at every somatic spike, so no trace outlasts the next step (\cref{sec:forget}).
Persistent Dendritic Memory (PDM) keeps the dendritic state through both.
Each channel has a retention time $\tau$, counted in environment steps, a decay $\lambda=e^{-1/\tau}$ and a phase $\theta$, and its state is a damped rotation driven by the current:
\begin{equation}
\begin{pmatrix} d_t \\ \tilde d_t \end{pmatrix}
= \lambda\, Q(\theta) \begin{pmatrix} d_{t-1} \\ \tilde d_{t-1} \end{pmatrix}
+ g(\lambda) \begin{pmatrix} I_t \\ 0 \end{pmatrix},
\label{eq:pdm}
\end{equation}
where $Q(\theta)$ is the rotation by the angle $\theta$ and $g(\lambda)$ is the write gain of \cref{sec:nwg}.
An integrator channel has $\theta=0$ and $\tilde d\equiv0$, so $d_t=\lambda d_{t-1}+g(\lambda)I_t$ is a leaky trace; a resonant channel is a damped oscillator, as in resonate-and-fire neurons~\cite{izhikevich2001resonate}.

\Cref{eq:pdm} is the only update of the channels: they keep their state across environment steps and through somatic spikes, the spikes of the layer enter only as current through $R$, and the state is cleared only at the start of an episode.
The influence of a current $k$ steps back, $g(\lambda)\lambda^{k}Q(k\theta)$, therefore does not depend on what the neuron fired in between, and the gradient along this path crosses no surrogate derivative.
The two kinds of channels store different aspects of the past.
An integrator stores what was seen, with inputs of all ages superposed under the smoothly decaying weights $\lambda^{k}$.
A resonator also stores when: an input of age $k$ appears at phase $k\theta$, which separates it from inputs of age $k\pm1$.

\subsection{Normalized Write Gain}
\label{sec:nwg}

Persistence alone does not make long retention times useful, since the gain with which a channel stores its input must not vanish as $\tau$ grows (\cref{sec:forget}).
The Euler update of \cref{eq:mcn} corresponds to $g=1-\lambda\approx1/\tau$, for which an input of unit variance gives the state a variance of $(1-\lambda)/(1+\lambda)\approx1/(2\tau)$: channels with long retention times store their input too faintly to drive the soma.
The Normalized Write Gain (NWG) sets
\begin{equation}
g(\lambda)=\sqrt{1-\lambda^2},
\label{eq:nwg}
\end{equation}
the normalization of the LRU~\cite{orvieto2023lru}.
For a normalized current of unit variance, the state then has a variance of $(1-\lambda^2)\sum_{k\ge0}\lambda^{2k}=1$ for every $\tau$.
With all channels on one scale, one layer can hold a wide spectrum of retention times: we initialize them uniformly on a log scale in $[1,1000]$ environment steps and learn them, so no time constant is tuned for a task.

\subsection{Stateless soma and policy input}
\label{sec:soma}

The soma reads the channels of its neuron and the direct input,
\begin{equation}
u_t = \textstyle\sum_{m=1}^{M} \big(c_m \odot d_{t,m} + \tilde c_m \odot \tilde d_{t,m}\big) + D x_t,
\label{eq:soma}
\end{equation}
where $c_m,\tilde c_m\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$ are weights of each neuron and $D$ is a synaptic matrix, and in simulation step $n=1,\dots,T$ it emits $s^{n}_t = \Hv\big(\LN(u_t) - n\vartheta/T\big)$ with $\vartheta=0.5$.
The soma keeps no state between environment steps, so no reset touches the memory, and its spike count encodes $u_t$ in $T+1$ levels instead of the single bit of one spike per step.
These binary frames are the only signal the neuron sends; the next layer and the feedback read their rate $\bar s_t$.
The synaptic operations $Bx_t$, $R\bar s_{t-1}$ and $Dx_t$ thus act on spikes, and the dendritic update is local to each neuron, with $O(NM)$ operations per step against $O(N^2)$ for the dense recurrent matrix of a GRU.

\paragraph{Policy input.}
The actor and critic read $z_t$, $h_t$ and the dendritic state of the second layer, and the Memory Maze probes read the same features.
The dendritic state leaves the neurons as spikes: each state variable, standardized over the layer, is compared with $T$ positive and $T$ negative thresholds $\pm(n-\tfrac12)\tfrac{2}{T}$, $n=1,\dots,T$, which gives $2T$ ON and OFF spike frames per environment step.
Supp.~\suppref{supp:policy}{E} compares a policy that reads $(z_t,h_t)$ only.

\paragraph{Training.}
The dendritic state is carried through training sequences and imagination rollouts and cleared only at episode starts, and all Heaviside steps are trained with the surrogate derivative of the world model.
