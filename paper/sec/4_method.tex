\Cref{sec:amnesia} gives a design rule: memory must live in a state that spikes never reset, written with a gain that does not vanish for long time constants.
We build \method from neurons that follow this rule, keeping the rest of the spiking world model unchanged (\cref{fig:overview}).

\subsection{Persistent dendritic neuron}
\label{sec:pdn}

A layer has $N$ neurons with $M$ dendritic channels each.
At environment step $t$ it receives the firing rate $\xx_t$ of its input spikes over the $T$ simulation steps and its own firing rate $\bar\ssp_{t-1}$ of the previous step.
The synaptic current into the dendrites is
\begin{equation}
\vect{w}_t = \LN\big(B\xx_t + R\,\bar\ssp_{t-1}\big)\in\mathbb{R}^{NM},
\label{eq:input}
\end{equation}
where $B$ and $R$ are synaptic matrices applied to spikes and $\LN$ is layer normalization~\cite{ba2016layernorm}.
Each channel has a retention time $\tau$, in environment steps, and a decay $\lambda=e^{-1/\tau}$.

\paragraph{Integrator channels} add the current to a leaky trace,
\begin{equation}
d_t = \lambda\, d_{t-1} + \sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}}\; w_t .
\label{eq:integrator}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Resonant channels} are damped oscillators with two state variables that rotate by a phase $\theta$ at every step:
\begin{equation}
\begin{pmatrix} a_t \\ b_t \end{pmatrix}
= \lambda \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix} a_{t-1} \\ b_{t-1} \end{pmatrix}
+ \sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}} \begin{pmatrix} w_t \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:resonant}
\end{equation}
An integrator channel is the special case $\theta=0$ with $b\equiv0$.

\paragraph{The soma} has no state. It reads the channels of its neuron and the direct input,
\begin{equation}
u_t = \textstyle\sum_{m=1}^{M} \big(C_m a_{t,m} + C'_m b_{t,m}\big) + D\xx_t,
\label{eq:soma}
\end{equation}
where $C_m, C'_m$ are weights of each neuron and $D$ is a synaptic matrix, and in simulation step $k$ it emits $s^{k}_t = \Hv\big(\mathrm{N}(u_t) - k\vartheta/T\big)$, $k=1,\dots,T$, with $\mathrm{N}$ the normalization used throughout the world model and $\vartheta=0.5$.
The number of spikes in an environment step thus encodes $u_t$ in $T+1$ levels.
These binary frames are the only signal the neuron sends; the next layer and the feedback read their rate $\bar s_t$.

\paragraph{Parameters.}
We use $N=512$ and $M=2$: one integrator and one resonant channel per neuron.
Retention times are initialized uniformly on a log scale in $[1,1000]$ environment steps and phases uniformly in $[0,2\pi)$ (the initialization of the LRU~\cite{orvieto2023lru}); both are learned.
The Heaviside step is trained with a surrogate derivative, and all states are reset only at the start of an episode.

\subsection{Why the neuron remembers}
\label{sec:why}

\paragraph{Persistent dendritic memory (PDM).}
Along the decay of a channel, the influence of a current $k$ steps back is $\partial d_t/\partial w_{t-k} = \sqrt{1-\lambda^2}\,\lambda^{k}$ for an integrator and $\sqrt{1-\lambda^2}\,\lambda^{k} R(k\theta)$ for a resonator, whatever the neuron fired in between.
This path never crosses a surrogate derivative, so the memory and its gradient decay with the retention time alone.
The feedback $R\bar\ssp_{t-1}$ adds a second path, through the threshold, that writes into the channels and never clears them; the memory holds without it, as the world model without feedback still recalls 64 steps and the exact lag of 32 steps (\cref{sec:ablation}).

\paragraph{Normalized write gain (NWG).}
For white input of unit variance, \cref{eq:integrator} gives $\mathrm{Var}(d_t)=(1-\lambda^2)\sum_{k\ge0}\lambda^{2k}=1$ for every $\tau$, whereas the Euler gain $1-\lambda$ of \cref{eq:mcn} gives $(1-\lambda)/(1+\lambda)\approx 1/(2\tau)$.
The LRU normalization~\cite{orvieto2023lru} thus keeps channels of every retention time on one scale; intervention G is the same scaling up to a factor of 2.

\paragraph{A spectrum of retention times.}
Retention times spread uniformly on a log scale cover memory lengths from one to a thousand steps, so no time constant is tuned per task.

\paragraph{Resonance tells time.}
An input that arrived $k$ steps ago contributes $\lambda^{k}(\cos k\theta,\ \sin k\theta)\,w_{t-k}$ to a resonant channel: its phase identifies the lag.
In integrator channels, inputs of different ages are superposed with smoothly decaying weights $\lambda^{j}$, so isolating the input of exactly $k$ steps ago from those of $k\pm1$ steps ago requires subtracting channels with nearby retention times, a poorly conditioned readout.
Integrators hold what was seen, and resonators also hold when it was seen.

\paragraph{Spike counts and feedback predict motion.}
One spike per step gives a downstream neuron one bit, a spike count $T+1$ levels; the feedback adds a nonlinear recurrence that linear dendrites lack.
On the same pixel walker episodes, the world model with both predicts the next frame as well as recurrent LIF neurons (image loss 44.3 vs.\ 44.1), and with one spike per step it does not (55.3; Supp.~\suppref{supp:diag}{F}).

\paragraph{Spikes communicate.}
All signals between neurons are spikes, so the synaptic operations $B\xx_t$, $R\bar\ssp_{t-1}$ and $D\xx_t$ are accumulates.
The dendritic recurrence and the readout are local to a neuron, and the normalizations need only the mean and variance of a layer, as in every spiking world model we compare: $O(NM)$ multiply-accumulates per step, against $O(N^2)$ for the dense recurrent matrix of a GRU.

\subsection{Spiking world model}
\label{sec:wm}

\method replaces the recurrent core of the spiking world model (\cref{sec:swm}) by two PDN layers; the second layer reads the spikes of the first, and its spikes form the deterministic state $h_t$.
Everything else is unchanged and spiking: the input layer that reads $(z_{t-1},a_{t-1})$, the encoder, the posterior and prior over $z_t$ (32 categorical variables with 32 classes), and the decoder, reward and continuation heads, all built from LIF neurons with $T=5$ simulation steps, trained with the losses of DreamerV3~\cite{hafner2025dreamerv3}.
The dendritic state is carried through training sequences and through imagination rollouts, and is cleared only at episode starts.

\paragraph{Policy input.}
The actor and critic read $z_t$, $h_t$ and the state of the dendrites of the second layer, on every task; the Memory Maze probes read the same features.
The soma sends at most $T$ spikes per neuron and step, whereas the dendrites hold the memory in graded form.
To pass them on as spikes, each state variable is standardized over the layer and encoded by $T$ ON and $T$ OFF thresholds at $\pm(k-\tfrac12)\tfrac{2}{T}$, $k=1,\dots,T$, giving $2T$ binary frames whose rates represent $2T+1$ levels.
With three state variables per neuron (the integrator and the two of the resonator), the actor and critic receive 3072 additional binary inputs per simulation step, the only path by which the dendritic state leaves a neuron; their first layer reads these inputs through a factor of rank 64.
Supp.~\suppref{supp:policy}{E} compares a policy that reads $(z_t,h_t)$ only.
